A water heater that quits on a January night in Shakopee is a real problem, not a nuisance. Our 24/7 line dispatches plumbers for gas and electric tank heaters that will not heat, leak, or make noise. If you see water on the floor, turn off the cold supply valve above the tank and shut off the gas or the breaker before you call.

Water heater repair means diagnosing why a tank is not delivering hot water and replacing the part that failed, such as a thermostat, heating element, thermocouple, gas valve or anode rod. A leaking tank itself cannot be patched, so that case moves to replacement.

Sort your problem first

No hot water at all

On an electric unit this is often a tripped breaker, a failed upper element or a bad thermostat. On gas it is often the pilot, the thermocouple or the gas control valve.

Lukewarm or runs out fast

A failed lower element, a sediment-packed tank or a broken dip tube. Hard water speeds up the sediment part.

Water around the base

Could be a loose fitting, a dripping relief valve or a failed tank. The plumber checks the source before saying which.

Emergency: a leaking or hissing water heater

Standing water at the base of a tank is the most urgent water heater call because it can ruin finished basement floors in an hour. Shut the cold water valve on top of the heater, switch off the power or gas, and keep towels from touching the burner area on a gas unit. If the leak is from a pipe fitting the repair is quick, but a tank that seeps from the bottom has failed and needs replacement.

A rotten-egg smell near a gas heater or any smell of gas is different. Leave the house, call the gas utility or 911 from outside, and then call us. We do not attempt work on a suspected gas leak until the utility has cleared it.

Why heaters fail in Shakopee

Shakopee Public Utilities lists its water at 21 grains per gallon. That is very hard water, and it drops scale on heating elements and settles sediment at the bottom of the tank. A heater that rumbles or pops when it fires is often working through that layer, and a flush or an element swap can add useful years.

If your tank is also past its usual life, a repair may not be the best use of money. The plumber will tell you the age from the serial plate and whether the part is worth buying.

Repair or replace: a quick rule

SituationUsually repairedUsually replaced
Tank under about 8 years oldThermostat, element, thermocouple, relief valveOnly if the tank wall leaks
Tank over about 12 years oldA cheap part with the tank otherwise soundRepeated repairs or visible rust at the base
Tank leaking from the bottomNo repair existsYes, on the same visit when stock allows

Hybrid, electric and gas units

Electric tanks fail at the elements and thermostats, and the fix is quick when parts are on the truck. Gas tanks add a burner, a thermocouple and a venting path, so the plumber also checks that exhaust is pulling correctly. Heat pump or hybrid tanks are less common in the area, and the plumber will say plainly if a repair is outside what the dispatched crew handles.

Plumbing code note

Relief valves, discharge pipes and venting on a water heater are governed by the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which Minnesota adopts in Rules chapter 4714, so a repair should leave those parts code compliant.
