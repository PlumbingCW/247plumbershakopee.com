Basement taking on water: do this first

If water is rising in the pit or on the floor, you have little time. Unplug anything in standing water only if the outlet is dry and you can do it safely, and never step into water near live power. Call the 24/7 line. A plumber can be dispatched at any hour, and same-day service is offered when available.

While you wait

  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker or GFCI has not tripped.
  • Lift the float to see whether the pump turns on, and listen for a motor hum.
  • Look for a frozen or blocked discharge line outside.
  • Move stored items off the floor and away from the pit.
  • Do not run a wet-dry vacuum in standing water near electrical outlets.

A sump pump sits in a pit at the lowest point of the basement and pumps water out through a discharge line when the pit fills. It is the main defense against water entering a basement, and it works only if the pump, float, check valve and discharge line are all in working order.

Why sump pumps matter in the Minnesota River valley

Shakopee sits in the lower Minnesota River valley, and the river runs high in wet years. In 2024 the flooding closed part of the Valleyfair parking lot and residents compared it with the 1965 flood, according to local news coverage. Low areas near the river have a higher water table, and a pump in those basements may run for long stretches during spring melt or a long rain.

New installs and backup systems

A new pump install starts with the pit. If the basement has no pit, the plumber cuts the floor and sets one, then adds a pump, a check valve and a discharge line sized for the flow. A battery backup pump is a second pump that runs on a deep cycle battery when power fails, and storms often take out power right when the sump needs it most.

Primary pump choice matters too. A vertical pedestal pump costs less but is louder and larger, while a submersible runs quietly in the pit. The plumber will recommend one based on the pit size and how often it runs.

Where the water goes

The discharge line should run outside and away from the foundation to a spot where water drains away, and not to a sanitary sewer line. Rules about where a sump may discharge vary, so ask the City of Shakopee building inspections office before changing the route. A discharge that ends at the foundation just recirculates and can make the pump run constantly.

Test the pump before the thaw

  • Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump starts and finishes.
  • Listen for grinding or rattling, and watch for water returning down the pipe.
  • Clean debris and silt from the pit once a year.
  • Check the outdoor end of the discharge to make sure it is clear.
  • Test the battery backup every few months and replace the battery on a schedule.

A pump that is old, noisy or short-cycles is a candidate for replacement before a storm, and not after. For water that comes in without a pump, see basement waterproofing.

Plumbing code note

Where a sump discharge may go is a code and city question under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, so confirm with the City of Shakopee.
