Sewage in the house: the first steps

Raw sewage coming up through a floor drain or tub is a health hazard and a full emergency. Stop all water use, keep children and pets away, and call the 24/7 line. A plumber can be dispatched at any hour, and same-day camera work is offered when available.

Sewer line repair means fixing a damaged section of the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the city main. The repair can be a short excavated spot repair, a trenchless liner pulled through the old pipe, or a full replacement, depending on what the camera shows.

How sewer lines fail

Root intrusion

Fine roots enter at joints and cracks, then thicken until water cannot pass.

Offset or separated joint

Soil shifts and two pipe sections no longer line up, which catches debris.

Belly or sag

A low spot holds water and solids, and the line clogs again and again.

Collapse or crack

Old clay or cast iron breaks, and soil washes in.

Corrosion or scale

Cast iron rusts from inside, and the pipe narrows.

Damage from digging

A cut or crushed section from a fence, tree or utility project.

Many Shakopee homes were built in the boom after the Bloomington Ferry Bridge opened in 1995, according to the city history page, and those laterals are often PVC. Older homes near downtown can have clay or cast iron. Material changes the failure mode, so the plumber reads the camera before suggesting a fix.

Choosing spot repair, lining or replacement

OptionFits whenTradeoff
Spot repairOne short broken or offset sectionNeeds a small excavation and then lawn or paving repair
Trenchless liningCracks or root damage along a longer stretch, pipe still roundCannot fix a collapsed pipe or a belly
Pipe bursting or replacementPipe collapsed, bellied or too corrodedMore digging or machine access needed

A localized crack with some root intrusion is a classic fit for a spot repair, while a long stretch of old clay with many root entries may justify lining or replacement. The plumber will show the video and explain why one option fits.

Trenchless pipe lining in plain terms

A lining job cleans the old pipe, then pulls a resin-saturated liner through and cures it in place, forming a new pipe inside the old one. It avoids trenching across a yard, driveway or finished basement. It needs a pipe that is still intact enough to carry the liner, so a camera and a cleaning pass come first.

Cleaning ahead of a liner is usually done by hydro jetting, and a camera pass confirms the pipe is ready. See sewer camera inspection for what that survey shows.

High groundwater and the Minnesota River valley

Shakopee sits in the lower Minnesota River valley, and high river years such as the 2024 flooding that closed part of the Valleyfair parking lot raise the water table in low areas. A sewer line with open joints can take in groundwater, and a heavy rain can then overload the line at the same time it is soaked. If backups appear only after storms, mention that on the call.

Who owns which part of the line

The line from your house to the city main is typically the property owner side up to a point, and ownership boundaries vary by city. Ask the City of Shakopee public works office where your responsibility ends before you assume a repair is a city job. A plumber can note on the camera where the private lateral meets the main.

If you are on a septic system

Most homes in Shakopee are on city sewer, but a few properties on the edge of the city may have a septic tank and drain field. That is a different system with different failures, so tell the dispatcher on the call and the right contractor is arranged.

What affects sewer line repair cost

Length of damageA short spot repair costs less than a full run

The plumber names the price after the camera shows the damage and before any digging starts. Many homeowner policies exclude sewer line damage from wear or roots, but some offer an add-on, so check yours. Call the 24/7 line to start. For a line that only needs clearing, see sewer line cleaning.

Plumbing code note

Sewer repair and replacement are plumbing work under the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, so ask the City of Shakopee about permits and inspection before digging.
