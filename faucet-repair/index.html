Emergency Faucet Repair in Shakopee, MN

A drip is a nuisance. A valve that will not shut off is an emergency. Call and we will sort which one you have.

  • 24/7 dispatch
  • Faucets and valves
  • Supply lines

Most faucet calls are drips, and those can wait for a regular visit. The faucet and valve calls that cannot wait are the ones where water will not stop, such as a stuck shutoff, a burst supply line under a sink or a broken tub spout. Our 24/7 line dispatches a plumber for those, and the fastest move on your side is to close the shutoff valve under the sink or the main valve for the house.

A valve that will not shut off

Old shutoff valves can seize, and turning them hard can snap the stem. If a valve will not close, do not force it. Shut the main valve and call. The plumber can replace the shutoff with a quarter turn valve so the next repair is easier.

A burst supply line under a sink can spray enough water to flood a kitchen in minutes. Shut the main, towel up the cabinet and call. Braided supply lines are a common failure point on older fixtures and are quick to replace.

A repair usually costs less than a new faucet, unless the finish is worn or the faucet is a model that is no longer made. The plumber will tell you honestly which side of that line your fixture is on.

Hard water wears faucet parts

Shakopee Public Utilities lists the city water at 21 grains per gallon. Mineral scale clogs aerators, binds cartridges and eats rubber seals. If you have a faucet that sputters or a handle that stiffens, scale is a common reason. Soaking an aerator in vinegar helps, and a softener, such as the one described on the water softener page, prevents it.

Tub and shower valves

A tub faucet that drips or a shower that will not shut off often has a worn cartridge inside the wall valve. The repair means shutting the water, removing the trim and replacing the cartridge. If the valve body is corroded or the access is behind tile, the work can be larger, and the plumber will explain before starting.

A showerhead that leaks at the arm or sprays unevenly is usually a gasket or plumber tape issue and quick to fix. Low flow from a showerhead is often scale in the screen.

Double sinks, drains and sink repair

Sink repair also covers leaking P-traps, loose drain flanges, sagging strainers and the slow drains that come with them. A kitchen double sink shares one trap arrangement, and the plumber checks both sides when one leaks. Disposal problems under the same sink are handled in garbage disposal repair.

Repair or replace

  • Repair when the body is sound, parts are available and the finish is good.
  • Replace when the faucet is pitted, discontinued or leaking from the body.
  • Replace the shutoff valves and supply lines whenever you replace the faucet.
  • Consider an upgrade if you want a pull-down sprayer or a touch faucet.

The plumber states the price for the fix before starting, and the price covers the parts that are named. Homeowner insurance usually pays for the water damage from a sudden burst line, but not the faucet itself. You can also read how toilet repair covers the other fixture most often called in.

Plumbing code note

Faucet and supply connections follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, including rules on backflow protection at some fixtures.

What to do if a faucet will not stop leaking

  • Close the shutoff stop under the sink, turning it clockwise.
  • Do not force a stuck stop, because it can snap and flood the cabinet.
  • Place a bucket under the drip and dry the cabinet floor.
  • Call if the stop will not close or water is spraying.
