Humming, jammed or leaking: what each means

Hums but does not spin

The blades are jammed. A hex wrench in the bottom socket can free it.

Silent, no hum

The unit has tripped its reset button or lost power.

Water under the sink

A leak from the top flange, a hose or the housing seal.

If water is leaking under your sink right now, close the shutoffs and turn off the disposal switch. A plumber can be dispatched around the clock, and the dispatcher explains timing on the call. A disposal that leaks from the housing itself is finished, because the seals inside cannot be replaced in the field.

Safe checks before calling

  • Turn the wall switch off, and unplug the unit or cut the breaker before touching anything.
  • Never put your hand in the disposal. Use tongs or pliers for anything you can see.
  • Insert a hex wrench in the bottom socket and work it back and forth to free a jam.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom after the unit cools.
  • If it hums again right away, stop and call.

Replacement and installation

A disposal typically lasts around ten years, and replacement makes sense when the housing leaks, the motor burns out or the unit is undersized for your use. Horsepower affects how well it handles food and noise, and the plumber can suggest a fit based on your sink and household. A disposal installation also involves an electrical connection, either a plug or a hardwired cord, and the discharge line.

For a first-time install, the plumber confirms there is an outlet or switch under or beside the sink, and that the drain can take the extra flow. Old kitchen drains that clog often should be cleared first, so see drain cleaning if the sink was already slow.

Dishwasher hookups and air gaps

The dishwasher drain hose connects either to the disposal inlet or to the sink drain with a high loop or air gap. A disposal swap means reconnecting that hose and knocking out the dishwasher plug in the new unit. A dishwasher that leaves standing water after a disposal install often has the knockout plug still in place.

What to keep out of the disposal

  • Grease and oil, which harden in the pipe past the disposal.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in quantity, which pack into sludge.
  • Fibrous vegetables such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell in water.
  • Bones and fruit pits, which can jam or dull the blades.

A note on hard water

Shakopee Public Utilities lists the city water at 21 grains per gallon. Scale builds inside the grinding chamber and on the drain connection, and cleaning the unit with ice and citrus peels is only cosmetic. If a disposal seems to work harder every year, scale on the shredder ring is a likely reason.

The plumber states a price for the repair or the new unit before beginning. Homeowner insurance may cover water damage from a sudden leak but not a worn disposal, so photograph the cabinet. A kitchen that also has a leaky faucet can be handled in the same visit through faucet repair.

Plumbing code note

Disposal drain connections, traps and dishwasher drain loops follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.

What to do if a disposal jams or leaks

  • Switch off the wall switch and unplug the unit before checking anything.
  • Never put a hand inside the grinding chamber.
  • Use a hex key at the bottom to free a jam, then press the reset.
  • Stop and call if water drips from the body of the unit.
