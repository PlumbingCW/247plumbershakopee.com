When the river rises: Savage in 2024

The Minnesota River crested in Savage during the 2024 flooding, one of the top five highest levels recorded there according to local news coverage. Even homes far from the water saw saturated ground and sump pumps that ran for days. That experience explains why Savage calls cluster around pumps, floor drains and sewer backups in wet years.

If the pit is full or the pump is silent, see sump pump repair first. If water enters without the pump failing, basement waterproofing is the longer-term answer, and a battery backup protects you when power drops with the storm.

Housing

Postwar quiet followed by fast suburban growth after the Highway 13 upgrade.

Dan Patch, Highway 13 and the newer homes

Marion Willis Savage bought the racehorse Dan Patch in 1902 and trained him at his farm, and the town took the Savage name in 1904. The city stayed small until Highway 13 was upgraded and the Bloomington Ferry Bridge opened in the mid-1990s, and the population reached 32,465 by 2020. Most Savage plumbing is therefore recent, with PEX, copper and PVC, while a few older homes near the river and downtown date from earlier decades.

Newer plumbing fails in newer ways. Builder-grade fixtures, supply lines under sinks and water heater parts account for many calls, so faucet repair and water heater repair are common visits.

Where Savage water comes from

Savage reports seven wells between 152 and 1,029 feet deep and buys water from Burnsville for about 71.5 percent of what it distributes. The city treats for iron and manganese, adds fluoride and uses chlorine to disinfect. The report we reviewed did not list a hardness figure, so test your tap or ask the City of Savage for its number before sizing a softener.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Savage

Steps while the crew is on the way

  • Close the main water valve to stop the flow at its source.
  • Cut power to the wet room at the breaker if outlets or the panel might get wet.
  • Move belongings off the floor and clear a path to the leak and the shutoff.
  • Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher you are in Savage, plus any cross street.

Sewer lines, roots and the Credit River

The Credit River flows north through the city to the Minnesota River, and mature trees along the creek corridors send roots into laterals. Repeat clogs, gurgling and a main line that backs up in rain are the calls we see most. A camera visit comes first through sewer camera inspection, and sewer line cleaning clears the line while a repair is planned.

Plumbing code note

Minnesota's plumbing code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and is administered by the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, so the same code applies in Savage as in Shakopee.
