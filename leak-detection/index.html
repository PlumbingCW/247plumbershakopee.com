Emergency Leak Detection in Shakopee, MN

Water bill up, meter spinning, or a stain growing on a ceiling. Call the 24/7 line and have the leak found before it is repaired.

  • 24/7 dispatch
  • Acoustic and thermal
  • High-bill diagnosis
Call (952) 866-9120

Most people calling about a leak do not yet know where it is. The meter is moving with everything off, the bill doubled, or a ceiling stain is spreading. Our 24/7 line sends a plumber to find the source, and if water is actively flowing, shut off the main valve first and keep power away from wet areas.

Leak detection means locating a hidden water leak without opening walls or floors at random. The plumber uses acoustic listening, pressure testing, thermal imaging and moisture readings to narrow the leak to a small area, then repairs only that spot.

Start with what you can see

Meter moves with fixtures off

This is the cleanest sign of a leak between the meter and the house, or inside the house.

Water bill jumps

A running toilet, a leaking irrigation valve or a hidden pipe leak can all raise usage fast.

Stain, bubble or warm floor

A ceiling stain points to a supply or drain leak above. A warm slab spot can mean a hot line leak.

Emergency: water you can hear or see right now

Water running inside a wall, ceiling or crawl space is urgent. Shut the main valve and call the 24/7 line. After-hours dispatch is available, and the plumber will bring listening and thermal tools to trace the source.

What to do while you wait

  • Shut the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms at the breaker if water is near outlets or fixtures.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest point to relieve pressure and drain the line.
  • Take photos of stains and the meter reading for the plumber and your insurer.

Meter still running, or a high water bill

Shakopee Public Utilities states that homeowners are responsible for all water used, including water lost through leaks, and for the service line from the home out to and including the connection to the water main. That makes the first step simple. Turn off every fixture, read the meter, wait fifteen minutes, then read it again. If it moved, there is a leak somewhere on your side.

A plumber can then isolate the main line from the house plumbing to see which side leaks. A leak between the meter and the house is a buried line leak and may need excavation. A leak inside the house is usually found with a listening probe and a thermal camera.

Slab, crawl space and under-house leaks

A warm spot or wet carpet on a slab floor can point to a leak under the concrete. Older homes around downtown Shakopee may have supply lines in a crawl space or basement ceiling that sweat or drip, and newer slab homes can have lines in the slab. Finding the exact spot lets the plumber open one small hole, not a whole floor.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

A ceiling stain after a rain may be a roof problem and not plumbing. A stain that grows with no rain and appears below a bathroom is more likely plumbing. A plumber checks supply and drain lines above the stain and tells you when a roofer is the right call.

Irrigation, yard and pool leaks

Irrigation systems can leak underground for weeks, and a spike in a summer bill is a clue. Shakopee Public Utilities runs seasonal watering restrictions from May 1 through September 30, so unusual use outside those rules can raise questions with the utility. Pool and spa leaks are a smaller part of this work and are handled as a one-off check.

You get a clear price before work starts. Many policies cover the water damage from a sudden leak but not the cost of finding or fixing a long-term one, so ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call the 24/7 line. If a leaking pipe has already burst, see burst pipe repair.

Plumbing code note

Once a leak is found, the repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and some repairs may need a permit from the City of Shakopee.
