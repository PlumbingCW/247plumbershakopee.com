Sewer backup: what to do first

A line that backs up right now is an emergency, and hydro jetting is often what a plumber reaches for when snaking failed or the line keeps clogging. Stop using water, do not flush, and call the 24/7 line. A plumber can be dispatched at any hour, and same-day service is offered when available.

Hydro jetting sends water through a hose and nozzle at high pressure to scour the inside of a pipe, which clears grease, sludge, soap scum and mineral scale that a cable would leave on the pipe wall. A camera check before and after shows what the water removed.

Jetting versus snaking

QuestionSnakeHydro jet
What does it doPokes through the clogCleans the full pipe wall

A plumber who hears that a cable got stuck partway down the line, or that the clog returns, will usually recommend a camera and then jetting. The camera also shows whether the pipe is sound enough for pressure.

Where jetting helps in Shakopee

Restaurants and food service near Shakopee Town Square and along Highway 169 build grease on drain lines every week, and jetting on a schedule keeps them open. Homes with older clay or cast iron laterals can have scale and roots at joints. Homes built in the 1990s and 2000s with PVC laterals more often clog from grease, wipes and bellies in the line.

Very hard water, listed by Shakopee Public Utilities at 21 grains per gallon, can also deposit scale inside drain lines over time, especially on lines that carry hot water from dishwashers and water heater drains. Jetting removes that layer while a cable would only cut through it.

What the visit looks like

  1. Camera first. The plumber looks at the line to find the clog and judge pipe condition.
  2. Set up at the cleanout. The hose goes in through a cleanout so nothing has to be pulled from a toilet.
  3. Jet from the clog outward. The nozzle works toward the main and pulls debris out.
  4. Check again. A second camera pass confirms the pipe is clean and notes defects.

When jetting is the wrong tool

High pressure can damage a pipe that is already cracked, offset or collapsed. If the camera shows a broken section, the answer is a spot repair or sewer line repair and not more pressure. A plumber who tells you that is helping you avoid a flooded basement.

Commercial lines and grease

Restaurants, shops and multi-unit buildings often need jetting on a schedule. Grease traps and interceptors are a separate service, but the lines leading to them are the part a jet can clear. See commercial plumbing for after-hours coverage on business properties.

Plumbing code note

Jetting does not change code requirements. Minnesota's adoption of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) in Rules chapter 4714 still governs any pipe repair that follows a camera inspection.
