Heat that goes out in January is an emergency in Minnesota, because a cold house can freeze pipes within hours. Our 24/7 line dispatches a plumber for hot water boilers that will not fire, leak or hold pressure. If you smell gas or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house first and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

No heat: what to check before the call

Quick checks that help the plumber

  • Confirm the thermostat is on heat and calling for it, and replace its batteries.
  • Check the boiler switch and the breaker or fuse for the boiler.
  • Look at the pressure gauge and compare it with the range on the boiler label.
  • See whether the pilot or display shows a fault code, and write it down.
  • Look for water at the base, pipes or valves.

A hot water boiler heats water and sends it through pipes to radiators, baseboard or in-floor heating. It runs a closed loop, so problems usually come from pressure, a failed circulator pump, a blocked burner, a bad sensor or an air-bound system.

Radiators and older systems

Many older Minnesota homes still use cast iron radiators fed by a hot water boiler. Those systems can run for decades but depend on working valves, air vents and a sound expansion tank. A radiator that heats only at the bottom or gurgles often has trapped air, and the plumber bleeds it as part of the visit.

Boiler pressure and the relief valve

Every boiler has a pressure relief valve that opens if pressure climbs too high. If it drips, do not cap it. That drip is a warning that the system is overpressured, the expansion tank has failed, or the valve itself is worn. A plumber diagnoses which and replaces the part.

Diverter and zone valves

Some systems use zone valves or diverter fittings to direct hot water between heating zones or between heating and a domestic hot water coil. A stuck valve can leave one zone cold while another overheats. These are replaceable parts, and a plumber confirms the wiring and flow before replacing one.

Hard water, scale and boilers

A closed boiler loop does not keep taking in fresh water, so scale from the city supply is less of a problem there than in a water heater. A leaking system that keeps adding fresh makeup water does bring in minerals each time. Shakopee Public Utilities lists the city water at 21 grains per gallon, so fixing the leak is worth more than topping off.

When to repair and when to replace

  • Repair when the boiler is under about 15 years old and the fault is a single part.
  • Replace when the heat exchanger is cracked or leaking internally.
  • Replace when repairs repeat within a season, or when parts are no longer made.
  • Ask about efficiency when the boiler is over 20 years old and repairs are large.

The plumber gives you the price before replacing a part. Most home policies do not pay to repair a boiler that failed from age, but can cover water damage from a sudden leak. Annual service helps catch failures before cold weather. For a hot water supply problem without a heating issue, see water heater repair.

Plumbing code note

Boiler piping, relief valves and the domestic water side follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, while gas and combustion rules add their own requirements.

What to do if the boiler stops heating

  • Check the thermostat, the power switch and the pressure gauge.
  • Do not open the boiler casing or touch the gas valve.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility.
  • Open cabinet doors on outside walls and keep the house warm until help arrives.
