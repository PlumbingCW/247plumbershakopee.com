A newer neighborhood with several kinds of housing

The City of Shakopee describes Windermere as a market-rate, mixed-use development built in phases in the Westchester neighborhood. The city says it includes single-family homes, townhomes and senior apartments, with several hundred units overall. The same neighborhood has seen new plats approved in stages since 2017.

That mix changes the first question you should ask during a plumbing problem. In a single-family house the plumbing is yours to manage. In a townhome or an apartment, the shutoffs, the shared walls and the repair responsibility can sit partly with an association or a landlord.

What public sources say about this area

The City of Shakopee Windermere page says the development is a multi-phase, mixed-use project in the Westchester neighborhood. It lists single-family homes, townhomes and senior apartments.

The same page lists a preliminary plat approved on April 18, 2017 and a final plat on July 18, 2017, an extension of 17th Avenue west of Marystown Road, a final plat for La Tour Terrace on October 1, 2020, and a preliminary plat for South Fifth Addition on May 18, 2021.

Minnesota plumbing work follows the Uniform Plumbing Code as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the City of Shakopee issues plumbing permits through its building inspections office.

What we do not know about your home

The city page gives plat dates and housing types, not pipe materials or builders' fixtures, so we make no claim about what is inside your walls. Homes built in different phases can differ, and the lot, builder and building type matter more than the neighborhood name. A plumber looks at the actual fixtures and lines.

General plumbing information for newer homes and townhomes

This section is general information, not a finding about Windermere. In a newer home, a builder or a warranty may still cover some defects, so check what paperwork you have before you pay for a repair. If you own a townhome, find out who owns the water shutoff for your unit and who handles a leak that crosses a shared wall.

Before you call about a leak in a townhome or apartment

  • Find the shutoff valve for your unit and test that it closes.
  • Ask your association or landlord who handles leaks that reach a neighbor.
  • Photograph the water and the damage, and note the time you found it.
  • Call the 24/7 line if the water is still running.

For a hidden leak, leak detection finds the source without opening more wall than needed. A new fixture or a moved line is a plumbing installation job, and your city issues a plumbing permit for many of them. A failing tank is a case for water heater replacement.

Plumbing code note

Minnesota's plumbing code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and is administered by the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, so townhome and apartment plumbing follows the same code as a detached house.
