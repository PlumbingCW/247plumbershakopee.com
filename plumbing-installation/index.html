Plumbing Installation in Shakopee, MN

From a new utility sink to a basement bathroom, a planned install is simpler when the plumber sees the layout first.

  • Fixtures and lines
  • Basement bathrooms
  • Vents and drains
Call (952) 866-9120

Plumbing installation is the work of adding or relocating fixtures, supply lines, drains and vents. It differs from repair because the plumber designs a layout, installs the lines in the right order and coordinates with inspections and other trades.

What we install

Kitchen

Sinks, faucets, dishwashers, disposals, pot fillers and ice maker lines.

Bathrooms

Toilets, vanities, tubs, showers, bidets and new rough-in for added baths.

Laundry and utility

Washer hookups, laundry sinks, hose bibs and sump discharge lines.

Basement bathrooms and finished lower levels

A basement bathroom needs a drain that is lower than the main, or a way to lift waste up to it. When the floor is below the sewer line, a sewage ejector pit and pump handles the toilet, and a grinder or up-flush unit is another option. The plumber checks the depth of the main before suggesting a layout, and a camera helps if the line is old. See sewer camera inspection before a big basement project.

Many homes in Shakopee have full basements, and a finished lower level is a common project. It adds living space, and also adds plumbing that has to work for decades inside finished walls, so the rough-in should be pressure tested before closing the wall.

Drain and vent basics

Every drain needs a vent so wastewater can flow and traps do not siphon dry. A new fixture usually ties into an existing vent stack or adds a new one through the roof or a tie-in. A fixture that gurgles or drains slowly after installation often has a vent problem and not a clog.

Plumbing codes set how far a fixture can sit from its vent, and a plumber follows those rules. Ask the City of Shakopee building inspections office about permits before you start.

Installing fixtures you buy yourself

The plumber can install faucets, toilets and sinks you supply, and will check the rough-in dimensions, supply lines and shutoff valves before starting. A fixture that arrives with a missing part can stall the job, so check the box before the plumber arrives. We also handle toilet installation and faucet installation as stand-alone visits.

A note on gas piping

If you smell gas at any time, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before you call a plumber. Gas piping for appliances is a safety-sensitive job, and we will tell you plainly if a specific gas job needs a different licensed trade.

Hard water and new plumbing

Shakopee Public Utilities lists the city water at 21 grains per gallon, so new fixtures will meet scale from the first day. Choosing fixtures with easy-to-clean aerators and planning for a softener at the same time can save work later. See water softener installation for more.

The plumber writes a scope and price after seeing the space, and any change is agreed before the work. Insurance does not pay for planned additions. For a leak behind a new install, leak detection can trace it.

Plumbing code note

New fixtures, vents and drains follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the Department of Labor and Industry administers it.
