Signs the main sewer line is clogged

A main line clog affects the whole house at once. Toilets gurgle when the washer drains, a shower fills your tub with gray water, or a basement floor drain bubbles. If any of that is happening now, stop using water and call the 24/7 line. A plumber is dispatched at any hour with a cable machine, and a jetter is available when the line calls for it.

Sewer line cleaning is the removal of blockages in the main drain, the large pipe that carries all of the house wastewater out to the street. It differs from clearing a sink because the pipe is longer, buried and usually reached from a cleanout.

EmergencySewage rising in the lowest tub, shower or floor drain, or a toilet that overflows when another fixture drains.

Finding the cleanout

Most homes have at least one cleanout, a capped pipe that gives direct access to the main. It is often in the basement near the stack, in the garage or outside near the foundation. Knowing where it is can save time, and a plumber can access the line without removing a toilet. If you cannot find one, the plumber will look for the best entry and may recommend adding a cleanout.

What clogs a sewer line

  • Roots. Fine roots enter at joints, thicken and catch paper and solids.
  • Grease. Cooking fat cools in the pipe and builds a layer that narrows it.
  • Wipes. Wipes sold as flushable often do not break up and form a mat.
  • Bellies. A sag holds water and solids, and the line clogs where it dips.
  • Foreign objects. Children, tools or construction debris can lodge in a line.

How the plumber clears it

The plumber starts with a cable machine and a cutting head sized to the pipe. The cable cuts through roots and paper, and then the plumber checks that water flows freely. If the line was only opened or grease remains, a hydro jet cleans the walls. A camera pass afterward shows whether the pipe is sound or needs sewer line repair.

Roots and the older lots around downtown

Mature trees are common in the older parts of Shakopee, and root intrusion is the most common cause of repeat main line calls on clay or cast iron laterals. A cable can cut roots back, but it does not stop regrowth. If the camera shows roots at the same joint twice, a lining or spot repair at that joint is cheaper than cleaning every year.

Cleaning versus repair

Cleaning is the right call when the pipe is whole and something is simply stuck in it. If the camera shows a collapse, a long offset or a deep belly, cleaning only buys time. A plumber who tells you that clearly is saving you the cost of a third clog.

Plumbing code note

Cleaning is maintenance, but any repair that follows is governed by the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which Minnesota adopts in Rules chapter 4714.
