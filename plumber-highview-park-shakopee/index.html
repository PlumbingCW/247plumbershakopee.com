General plumbing information for a newer home

This section is general advice and not a finding about Highview Park. Read your builder warranty and any home warranty before you book a repair, because a covered defect should go to the party who owes the work. If the water is actively flooding a room, shut the main valve and call first, then sort out who pays afterward.

First steps when something fails in a newer home

  • Close the main water valve and photograph the water and the damage.
  • Find your warranty paperwork and note the dates and contact numbers.
  • Ask the plumber to write down the cause, so a warranty claim has proof.
  • Keep any failed part if the plumber removes it.

A fixture that leaks soon after move-in is a case for faucet and valve repair or a check of the connections under the sink. Drains that gurgle or back up after the first months call for drain cleaning and sometimes a camera inspection. If you want a new line or fixture added, plumbing installation covers it.

New homes still run on city water at 21 grains per gallon, per Shakopee Public Utilities, so scale starts early in a new tank. A water softener is a choice to price while the house is new. A water heater repair call is usually a sediment or thermostat question in the first years.

Plumbing code note

Minnesota's plumbing code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, adopts the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and is administered by the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry, so repairs in a new home follow the same code and may need a permit.
