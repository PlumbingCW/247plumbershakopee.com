If the toilet is overflowing right now

  1. Lift the tank lid. Push the flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl.
  2. Shut the supply valve. Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  3. Keep the water off the floor. Put towels down and move rugs, and keep power away from wet outlets.
  4. Call the 24/7 line. A plumber can be dispatched at any hour, and the dispatcher tells you what timing is possible.

Toilet repair covers fixing a toilet that clogs, overflows, leaks, runs or rocks, and replacing the toilet when the fixture is cracked or beyond repair. Many fixes are small parts such as a flapper, fill valve or wax ring, but a leak at the floor can mean hidden damage.

A toilet that will not stop running

A running toilet wastes water quietly. The flapper may not seal, the fill valve may stick or the chain may be tangled. Shakopee Public Utilities notes that homeowners pay for all water used, including water lost through leaks, so a toilet that runs all night appears on your bill. A new fill valve and flapper are among the least expensive plumbing repairs.

To test for a silent leak, put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait fifteen minutes without flushing. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

Leaks at the base

Water around the base usually means the wax ring or the flange has failed, or the closet bolts are loose. The plumber lifts the toilet, checks the flange for cracks or a bad height, replaces the wax ring and sets the toilet back. If the subfloor is soft, the repair also includes inspecting it, because a slow leak can rot wood for months.

Clogs and when they mean more

A plunger clears most single toilet clogs. If the toilet clogs again right away, or the shower gurgles when you flush, the problem is likely in the main line and not the toilet. In that case drain cleaning is the right next step, and a toilet auger is not.

Hard water and toilet parts

Mineral scale in very hard water can foul fill valves and clog the small ports under the rim. Shakopee Public Utilities lists the city supply at 21 grains per gallon, so tank parts here may need earlier replacement than the package suggests. A vinegar soak helps the rim jets in between visits.

Installing a new toilet

A replacement is worth it when the bowl is cracked, the toilet is old and uses far more water per flush than current models, or the repair parts cost nearly as much as a new one. The plumber removes the old fixture, checks the flange, sets the new toilet on a fresh wax ring and tests the flush. Bring the rough-in measurement from the wall to the closet bolts if you buy the toilet yourself.

The plumber states the price for parts and labor before opening the tank or lifting the bowl. Insurance usually covers the water damage from a sudden overflow, but not wear to the toilet itself, so photograph the damage. See also faucet repair and leak detection if you suspect another hidden leak.

Plumbing code note

Toilet installation details such as flange height and venting come from the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.
