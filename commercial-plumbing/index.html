A plumbing failure at a business costs more than the repair, because customers cannot be served while water is on the floor. Our 24/7 line takes after-hours calls from restaurants, shops, offices and property managers in Shakopee, and a plumber is dispatched with the equipment needed. If water is spreading, shut the main valve and keep staff out of wet electrical areas while you wait.

Common after-hours calls

Floor drain overflow

Kitchens, restrooms and mechanical rooms back up when a line clogs or a lift pump fails.

Pipe leaks above ceilings

A supply or drain leak above a tile ceiling can ruin stock and finishes fast.

Restroom failures

Flush valves, toilets and urinals that will not stop running or will not drain.

Businesses we see around Shakopee

Shakopee has a mix of retail near the Shakopee Town Square, restaurants and offices downtown, and light industrial buildings along the Highway 169 corridor. Each has its own plumbing profile. A restaurant lives or dies on its grease lines and floor drains, a retail strip shares a building sewer among tenants, and a warehouse may have large roof drains and a few restrooms.

Because the area also draws visitors to Valleyfair, Canterbury Park and Mystic Lake events, restroom and food service demand spikes on event weekends, so a plumbing failure then is especially costly. Plan ahead with the maintenance steps below.

Floor drains, lift pumps and grease lines

A commercial floor drain that overflows often has a clogged line downstream or a failed lift pump in a below-grade space. The plumber checks the pump, the float and the discharge line, then clears the drain with a cable or hydro jetting. Grease lines to an interceptor are a regular maintenance item, and cleaning the trap itself is a separate service that a plumber can schedule or refer.

Finding a leak in a commercial building

Commercial leak calls are among the hardest to price because the pipe may be above a ceiling, in a chase or under a slab. The plumber uses acoustic and thermal tools, and the leak detection page explains the process. Be ready with building plans or a drawing of the water shutoffs if you have one.

Backflow testing

Many commercial buildings have backflow preventers on water lines, irrigation or fire systems that protect the public supply. Water utilities and cities often require periodic testing for certain connections. Ask the City of Shakopee or Shakopee Public Utilities whether your account requires it, and a plumber can then test the device and provide the result for filing.

Property managers and tenants

Property managers can call on behalf of tenants and give the unit number, the contact name and the access instructions. The plumber will send the findings and a written price for the work to the contact you name. For multi-unit buildings, a single shared main clog can affect several units, so tell us how many are affected.

A short maintenance checklist

Reduce the odds of a shutdown

  • Know where the main water shutoff and every floor drain are, and label them.
  • Schedule grease line cleaning on a fixed calendar, not after a backup.
  • Test sump and lift pumps twice a year and keep spare floats.
  • Walk restrooms weekly for running flush valves and slow drains.
  • Keep the 24/7 number posted for the opening and closing managers.

Plumbing code note

Commercial fixtures, grease handling and backflow rules are set by the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, plus any local requirements for your building.

What to do when a business has a leak or backup

  • Close the water shutoff and stop using affected fixtures.
  • Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and sewage.
  • Cover or move stock and equipment out of the water path.
  • Call the 24/7 line and give the building address and the shutoff location.
