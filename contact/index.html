What to tell the dispatcher

Have these ready when you call

  • Your address and the nearest cross street.
  • What the water is doing, for example spraying, dripping or backing up.
  • Whether you have shut off the main valve, and whether you can find it.
  • Whether anyone is in danger, such as water near outlets or a gas smell.
  • A good number to reach you if the call drops.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility before you call us. If water is spreading near the electrical panel, stay out of the water and call the line from a dry spot.

Why we do not use a contact form

A plumbing problem rarely waits for an email. Calling lets the dispatcher ask a few questions, give you safety steps and send a plumber right away, which a form cannot do. If you are not in a hurry, you can email us and we will reply as soon as we can, but for anything wet, call.
